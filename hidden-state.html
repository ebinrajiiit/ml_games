You are the recurrent weight

A one-number RNN: ht = tanh(wxh·xt + whh·ht−1)

Think of ht as the score you keep in your head while following a cricket match on the radio. After every ball (xt) you update it. whh is how much of the old score you carry forward; wxh is how much the new ball counts.

Ten rounds, about six minutes:

  1. Rounds 1–5, tune the memory. Move the two sliders until your hidden state (solid line) traces the target (dots).
  2. Round 6, keep the gradient alive. Choose whh so a gradient survives 40 steps back.
  3. Rounds 7–10, quick questions. Ten seconds each.

Each round is worth 10 points. Write your slider values in worksheet Part D.

What to take from it

0 < whh < 1: a pulse fades step by step, like a fixed deposit that loses a little every year. The closer to 1, the longer it lasts.

whh < 0: the state flips sign every step.

Steady input piles up until tanh flattens it near ±1.

The gradient only survived 40 steps for whh between about 0.944 and 1.059. Training a plain RNN means finding that thin band, for every unit. The LSTM gets around it in the next lecture.
